# Assyrian Reader: build spec

As of 13 November 2026. Phase 1 of the roadmap is built in this repository.

## Purpose and learner

Assyrian Reader teaches a fluent speaker of Iraqi Assyrian to read and write the Eastern (Madnhaya) script. The learner already knows the words by ear, so the app never teaches meaning or pronunciation from scratch. It teaches the link between sounds the learner knows and letters and marks the learner does not.

That sets three design rules:

- **Reading first.** Every exercise starts from script and ends at a word the learner recognises.
- **No audio dependency.** The app works with no sound at all. The learner can add their own voice, and nothing requires it.
- **The learner is the dialect authority.** Where the family's pronunciation differs from the written standard, the learner records their own version and the app keeps it.

## What the app does today

79 cards: 22 letters, 11 marks and 46 words. The original single-page prototype is kept at `reference/prototype.html`.

| Area | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Letters | All 22 letters in script order, right to left. Each opens to its sound, its joined shapes, its marked variants and up to three known words that use it. |
| Marks | Seven vowel marks and four other marks (soft dot, silent line, plural dots, tilde), each with a one-line rule. |
| Words | 46 words in eight groups. Each opens to a letter-by-letter sounding-out, generated by rule from the spelling. |
| Practice | Rounds of about ten multiple-choice cards. New items are introduced before they are tested. A missed card returns three cards later. |
| Lesson order | Computed, not hand-written. A word is introduced as soon as every letter and mark in it has been met. The first word arrives after two letters and one mark. |
| Corrections | Per word, the learner can save their own pronunciation and flag a spelling. The footer exports these as JSON. |
| Voice | The app comes with a built-in voice: a computer voice from Syriac.IO (Nineveh Plains) for every letter, mark and word except the letter E. Pressing a letter, mark or word says it, a small loudspeaker marks everything that will speak, and in practice the clip plays when a card is introduced and when it is answered. The learner can record their own clip over any card, in its sheet or in a recording session that shows one card at a time, prompted in English. Their own clip then plays instead, and deleting it brings the built-in voice back. Own clips stay on the device; the footer saves them to one file and loads such a file, which is how a recorded voice reaches another phone or tablet. |

Every word spelling is checked by machine: a rule-based reading of each spelling must reproduce its Wiktionary transliteration. All 46 pass.

Not built: writing or tracing practice, sentences, grammar, numbers above five, and typing in script.

## Content model

All content lives in three record types under `content/`. The app's logic reads only these records, so adding a word never needs a code change.

A word record:

```json
{
  "id": "shawra",
  "script": "ܫܲܒ݂ܪܵܐ",
  "translit": "šaḇrā",
  "reading": "shawra",
  "english": "child",
  "category": "people",
  "source": "wiktionary-swadesh",
  "checked": false
}
```

| Record | Fields | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Word | id, script, translit, reading, english, category, source, checked | `script` carries every vowel mark. `translit` is the source's scholarly form. `reading` is a plain-letter version for the learner. `checked` is true only once a fluent reader has confirmed the spelling. |
| Letter | id, char, name, sound, note, joins, variants | `joins` is `right` for the eight letters that never join to the next letter, otherwise `both`. |
| Mark | id, sample, name, effect, note, group | `sample` shows the mark on a carrier letter. |
| Learner state | per card: box, due, seen. Per word: own pronunciation, spelling flag | Kept apart from content so content can be replaced without losing progress. |
| Voice clip | per card: sound format and the recorded bytes | The learner's own data. Stored on the device under `assyrian-reader-clip:<card id>`, apart from the learner state. Never in the repository. |

Three things are derived at run time and never stored:

- **The sounding-out.** A word is split into clusters, each a base letter (U+0710 to U+072C) plus the marks on it, and each cluster is read by rule.
- **What a word requires.** The letters and marks found in its spelling.
- **The lesson order.** See Learning engine.

**Validation rule for the build:** reading `script` by rule must reproduce `translit`, ignoring vowel length and doubled consonants. A word that fails does not ship. The rule is enforced by `tests/content.test.ts`.

## Learning engine

A card is one letter, one mark or one word, and every card moves through six boxes numbered 0 to 5.

**Lesson order.** Repeatedly pick the word that needs the fewest items not yet introduced. Introduce those letters and marks, then the word. Letters and marks used by no word go last. This gives Alap, Nun and the long-a mark, then the word for "I", then Beth, then the word for "father".

**A round.**

1. Take up to 10 cards that are due, lowest box first.
2. Add up to 4 new cards, fewer when reviews already fill the round.
3. Show new cards in pairs: introduce two, then test those two.
4. Fill to about 10 questions with extra passes on the new cards and the weakest known cards.

**Grading.**

- Right: up one box, to a maximum of 5.
- Wrong: back to box 1, and the card repeats three cards later.
- A right answer on a repeat does not promote the card.
- Wait before the next review: 10 minutes at box 1, then 1, 3, 7 and 21 days at boxes 2 to 5.
- A card counts as known well from box 4.

**Question types.**

| Card | Question | Wrong options drawn from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letter | Shown the letter, pick its name and sound | Its look-alike first (Dalath and Resh, Beth and Kap, Waw and Qop, E and Lamadh, Nun and Yodh), then random letters |
| Letter, from box 1 | Given the name and sound, pick the letter | Same |
| Mark | Shown the mark, pick what it does | Other marks |
| Word | Shown the script, pick the word | Words starting with the same letter first, so the first letter alone is not enough |
| Word, from box 2 | Given the word, pick its script | Same |

## Look and feel

The app is themed in the colours of the Assyrian flag and built to suit a child as well as an adult.

- **Colours.** Blue, red, gold and white. The header is a blue band with the language's name in gold, the flag's star-and-sun emblem beside the title, and red and white waves along its bottom edge. Red is decoration only: a wrong answer is shown in orange, a right one in green, so the flag's red never reads as a mistake.
- **Shapes.** Large rounded tiles and buttons with a coloured bottom edge that sink when pressed. Buttons are at least 52 pixels tall.
- **Type.** Fredoka for headings and buttons, Nunito for running text, both bundled. The script font is unchanged.
- **Rewards.** Progress is shown as four-pointed stars: pale until a card is met, blue once started, gold once known well. A finished round shows one to three stars (one for finishing, two from 60% right, three from 90%) and a short burst of confetti. The stars are worked out from the round, never stored.
- **Wording.** Short and encouraging. A miss says the card will come back, never that the learner failed.
- **Motion.** Every animation is switched off when the device asks for reduced motion. Light and dark both follow the device setting.

All decoration lives in `src/components/Decor.tsx` and carries no script.

## Technical approach

An installable web app: one codebase, works offline, sits on the phone's home screen, and needs no server for version 1. It can be wrapped as a store app later.

| Concern | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Stack | TypeScript, React and Vite, built as a progressive web app. Content as JSON files in the repository. |
| Script font | Noto Sans Syriac Eastern (SIL Open Font Licence) is bundled in the app. Do not rely on the device's own Syriac font, which may be missing or draw marks differently. |
| Line height | At least 1.6 on script, because marks stack above and below the letters. |
| Direction | `dir="rtl"` and `lang="syr"` on every script run, isolated from the left-to-right interface text around it. |
| Joining | Never wrap part of a word in its own styled element: it can break the joins between letters. To highlight a letter, draw each cluster in its own box, as the sounding-out does. |
| Text handling | Walk strings by code point. Store spellings exactly as sourced. Compare code point by code point in tests. |
| Storage | Local first (IndexedDB, with localStorage as a fallback). Optional account sync later. The notes export uses the same shape as the prototype's. |
| Content import | Phase 2: a script that reads the kaikki.org extract, keeps Assyrian Neo-Aramaic entries (`lang_code` `aii`) that have a vowelled headword and a romanisation, runs the validation rule, and writes the word file with source and licence fields. |
| Tests | The spelling validator; lesson order covers every card exactly once; scheduler unit tests for each grading rule; a rendering test that plays a whole round. |

## Roadmap

Each phase has a test that must pass before the next begins.

1. **Port.** Done. The prototype is rebuilt here with content in JSON and the tests above.
2. **More words.** Import 300 to 500 everyday words from Wiktionary: family, food, home, church, numbers to 100, days and months. Done when every word passes the validator and a fluent reader has spot-checked at least 50.
3. **Beyond single words.** Phrases and short sentences, the one-letter words that attach to the next word, and plural forms. Done when the sounding-out handles a full sentence.
4. **Writing.** Letter tracing, an on-screen Assyrian keyboard, and typing a word from its sound. Done when a word can be typed with all its marks.
5. **Audio.** Built, ahead of its turn: a built-in voice ships with the app and plays offline, the learner can record their own clip over any card, and a voice file carries those clips to other devices. Still to do: have a fluent speaker check the built-in voice, and replace the clips that are wrong.

## Risks and open questions

The main risk is teaching a spelling or a rule that a fluent reader would not accept. Each item below needs a decision or a check before the word list grows.

- **Written standard against everyday speech.** Spellings follow the written standard, which keeps sounds that everyday Iraqi speech drops or changes. Open question: should the app show the standard reading, the learner's own, or both by default?
- **Community-edited source.** Wiktionary can be wrong. Second-source every word against the Sureth Dictionary, and have a fluent reader review the list. Open question: who is that reader?
- **Four words outside the Swadesh list.** The words for hello, God, house and king came from individual Wiktionary entries and the orthography notes, not the checked list. They pass the validator but have had no second look.
- **Names and descriptions written from general knowledge.** The letter names, mark names and the descriptions of each mark's shape were not copied from a source. Check them against an Assyrian school textbook.
- **Soft letters.** The app reads the soft dot on Dalath and Taw as dh and th, and tells the learner their family may say d and t. Confirm how the learner's family says them.
- **Reuse terms not read.** The Sureth Dictionary and SargonSays terms of use have not been opened. Treat both as lookup-only until they are.
- **Not yet seen on a device.** The screens are covered by rendering tests but nobody has looked at the app on a phone. Check the script draws and joins correctly before building further.
- **The built-in voice is unchecked and machine-made.** No fluent Nineveh Plains speaker has listened to it, and Syriac.IO calls its model a work in progress. Three known weak spots: the letter E has no clip because the one generated was silent; the letter clips were generated from the letter names in English spelling ("Kheth", "Lamadh"), which the voice may misread; and the clips for the two marks Rwasa and Rwaha sound almost the same. The words were generated from our exact spellings and are the safest part. The footer tells the learner to record over anything that sounds wrong.
- **No written permission for the voice.** Syriac.IO publishes no terms for reusing its audio. The owner chose to include it as free educational use. Sending a courtesy note to info@syriac.io is still worth doing.
- **Recording not tried on a phone.** Recording and playback are tested in desktop Chromium with a stand-in microphone. Safari on iPhone records in a different sound format and has not been tried.
